# Academic Writing Refiner: Reviewer Response & Rebuttal Guide

This guide provides strategies, tone guidelines, and templates for crafting winning peer-review rebuttal letters and responses to editors and reviewers.

---

## 1. Golden Rules of Rebuttal Writing

1. **Answer Every Comment Directly**: Never group distinct critical comments together to avoid answering hard questions. Number each point sequentially.
2. **Be Respectful, Professional & Calm**: Even if a reviewer misunderstood or was aggressive, remain objective, thank them for identifying the ambiguity, and clarify the manuscript text.
3. **Show, Don't Just Tell**: Always quote the exact revised text from the manuscript and provide precise page and line numbers:
   - *"We agree with the reviewer and have added the following explanation on page 7, lines 142–150: [Quote]"*.
4. **Three-Part Response Structure**:
   - **Step 1: Acknowledgment & Agreement**: *"We thank the reviewer for this constructive suggestion / insightful observation."*
   - **Step 2: Action Taken**: *"In response, we have conducted an additional sensitivity analysis / revised Section 3.2 to clarify..."*
   - **Step 3: Concrete Evidence & Citation**: Provide the new table, figure, statistical result, or exact quoted manuscript snippet.

---

## 2. Standard Rebuttal Response Templates

### A. When Agreeing with the Reviewer and Adding New Analysis/Text
> **Reviewer Comment 1:** *"The authors should clarify how missing data were handled in the longitudinal cohort."*
>
> **Response:**
> We thank the reviewer for pointing out this omission. We used multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE) under the assumption of missing at random (MAR), generating 20 imputed datasets. We have updated the Methods section (**Page 5, Lines 112–119**) as follows:
>
> > *"Missing baseline and follow-up covariates were handled using multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE, 20 imputations), assuming data were missing at random. Sensitivity analyses using complete-case analysis yielded consistent odds ratios (Supplementary Table S3)."*

### B. When Respectfully Disagreeing with a Reviewer Suggestion
> **Reviewer Comment 2:** *"The authors should re-analyze the trial using an unadjusted t-test rather than ANCOVA."*
>
> **Response:**
> We appreciate the reviewer's perspective regarding the simplicity of a two-sample t-test. However, per European Medicines Agency (EMA) and ICH E9 statistical guidelines, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) adjusting for baseline values is the recommended primary analysis for randomized trials as it significantly increases statistical power and corrects for baseline imbalances (Vickers & Altman, BMJ 2001). 
> 
> To address the reviewer's concern, we have now included the unadjusted t-test results as a supplementary sensitivity analysis in **Supplementary Table S4**, which demonstrates that the conclusions remain unchanged ($p = 0.006$ unadjusted vs. $p = 0.002$ adjusted). We have added clarifying text on **Page 6, Lines 140–145**.

---

## 3. Formatting the Rebuttal Document

- Use clear typography to separate reviewer comments from author responses:
  - **Reviewer comments**: Bold or italicized blockquotes.
  - **Author response**: Plain text with clear heading.
  - **Quoted revisions**: Indented text in quotation marks with highlighted or blue text for changed lines.
