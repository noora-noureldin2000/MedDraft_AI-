# Section-by-Section Conventions

## Abstract

Self-contained: problem, approach, key result **with numbers**, significance — in ~150–250 words (medical structured abstracts 250–350 words: Background / Methods / Results / Conclusion). No citations, no undefined acronyms, no data absent from the paper. Write it last.

## Introduction

Problem → gap → contribution → brief results → paper outline. The reader should grasp what was done and why it matters within the first page.
- Medical 3-move funnel (~500–700 words): Territory (burden with 1–2 authoritative citations per claim) → Gap (hinge word: However, Nevertheless, Despite, To date, Yet; specify WHAT is unknown, in WHOM, why it matters) → Purpose (strong verb: determine, evaluate, compare, quantify).
- Never a weak gap ("More research is needed", "Little is known") without specification.

## Related Work / Literature Review

Group by theme, not by paper. Each paragraph ends by distinguishing the current work from what was just discussed. Avoid laundry-list style ("X did A. Y did B. Z did C."). End with a synthesis paragraph stating what is known, unknown, and why the present work matters.

## Methodology / Methods

Logical order: design → participants/population → intervention/approach → outcomes → data collection → analysis. Define notation, terms, and instruments before use. Medical: six subsections in chronological order (design, participants with criteria, intervention with generic names, outcomes, data collection, statistical analysis with sample-size calculation, named tests, missing-data handling, software + version) plus the applicable reporting checklist (CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA 2020, STARD, CARE). CS: equations for precision plus intuition in words alongside them.

## Results / Experiments

- Medical: data only — zero interpretation, comparison, or speculation. Flow: participant flow → baseline → primary outcome (effect size + CI + p-value) → secondary outcomes (all, even if non-significant) → adverse events. Each datum in exactly ONE format (text or table or figure).
- CS: lead with research questions or hypotheses, then setup, then results. Tables and figures self-contained with descriptive captions. Every performance claim carries a number plus a citation or experimental reference.

## Discussion

Inverted funnel: answer the research question in the first sentence (with key numbers) → compare with agreeing literature, then conflicting literature (discrepancies framed as population, methodological, power, or measurement differences) → evidence-labeled mechanisms ("Our data demonstrate…" / "These findings suggest…" / "A possible mechanism is…" / "We speculate that…") → strengths and limitations (State → Impact → Mitigate; candid, never apologetic) → conclusion with numbers, one implication, one specific future direction. Never end with bare "more research is needed".

## Conclusion

Summarize contributions (not the whole paper), acknowledge limitations honestly, suggest concrete future directions. Keep it to 3–5 sentences in short papers: summary with numbers, implication, specific next step.

## Cover Letter and Response to Reviewers

Cover letter, four paragraphs: hook (main finding in one sentence) → why it matters → why this journal (cite its recent articles) → closing (originality, approvals, no competing interests). No hype, no flattery, correct journal name.
Reviewer responses: number every comment, answer each one politely, show changes with page/line numbers (correct → thank and change; partially correct → thank and clarify; incorrect → thank, evidence, compromise).
