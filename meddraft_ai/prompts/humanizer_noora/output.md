Knee osteoarthritis is a chronic degenerative joint disorder and a major contributor to disability-adjusted life years worldwide (1,2). The condition involves progressive cartilage breakdown, synovial inflammation, and subchondral bone remodeling. Patients experience chronic pain, stiffness, and reduced function as a result (3,4). Prevalence has doubled since the mid-20th century, driven by rising obesity and longer life expectancy (5,6). OA also has systemic effects beyond the joint. It increases morbidity and worsens mental well-being (4,7). Traditional drugs like NSAIDs are limited by modest efficacy and serious cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, and renal risks (8,9). Therefore there is a clear need for safer nutraceutical alternatives (4,7).

Resveratrol is a polyphenolic phytoalexin derived from Polygonum cuspidatum. Interestingly it exhibits anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and chondroprotective properties (3,8). Its primary mechanism involves activating SIRT1, which regulates chondrocyte metabolism and suppresses inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and TNF-α (1,10). On one hand some RCTs report significant improvements in WOMAC and VAS scores when resveratrol is combined with meloxicam (7,8). On the other hand the ARTHROL trial found no meaningful pain reduction over placebo. This discrepancy may reflect insufficient bioavailability or advanced structural damage in older cohorts (9,11). Performance-based outcomes such as grip strength and gait speed have been inconsistently reported across studies (2,6). Dosing protocols are also highly variable, ranging from a 40 mg loading schedule to 500 mg daily, which complicates the formulation of standardized guidelines (8,12). Long-term data remain limited although a 24-month trial in postmenopausal women showed sustained improvements in pain and overall well-being (13).

Therefore this systematic review and meta-analysis sets out to synthesize current RCT evidence on resveratrol for knee OA. We aim to quantify its effects on pain, joint function, performance-based metrics, and biochemical markers including SIRT1 and C-reactive protein. Ultimately we seek to determine whether resveratrol represents a viable therapeutic option across the clinical spectrum of OA and to inform future clinical practice and research directions.
