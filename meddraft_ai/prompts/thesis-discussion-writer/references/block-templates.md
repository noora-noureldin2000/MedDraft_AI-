# Block Templates and Phrase Banks

Fill-in templates for every deliverable block. Use them as skeletons: never ship a template sentence with placeholder brackets still in it, and never let two consecutive paragraphs open with the same template.

---

## 1. Introduction to Discussion (200–250 words, 3 paragraphs)

**Paragraph 1 — burden (2–4 sentences, present tense, cited):**
```
[Condition] represents a [critical/growing/persistent] [global/regional] health
challenge, affecting approximately [prevalence with range] of [population] (refs).
In [comorbid/specific population], the prevalence of [condition] is markedly
elevated, frequently exceeding [X]%, due to shared pathophysiological pathways
involving [mechanism 1] and [mechanism 2] (refs). While [current gold standard]
remains the diagnostic cornerstone, its routine application is restricted by
[barriers], necessitating [what is needed] (refs).
```

**Paragraph 2 — evidence and gap (3–4 sentences, hinge word required):**
```
[Emerging tool/index] has emerged as a [cost-effective/promising] [surrogate
marker/approach] with significant potential for [target use] (refs).
[Meta-analytical/pooled] evidence indicates that [tool] possesses [performance
metric], yet data specifically evaluating its performance in [target population]
remain limited (refs). Furthermore, [inconsistency/open question 1] (refs), and
[open question 2] (refs), complicating [clinical implementation/interpretation].
```

**Paragraph 3 — the present study (2–3 sentences):**
```
The present study addresses these research gaps by [evaluating/comparing]
[approach] specifically in [population]. Utilizing a [design] at [setting], we
[compared/examined] [groups/measurements] to [analytic goal]. This research aims
to [validate/determine] [primary aim] and [secondary aim] in this [high-risk/
specialized] population.
```

Rules: no results, no new literature beyond gap support, do not copy the thesis Introduction sentence-for-sentence.

## 2. Findings paragraph (100–150 words)

```
[Domain opener] in the present study [revealed/demonstrated/identified]
[finding 1 with exact statistics]. [Finding 2 with statistics; finding 3;
non-significant results stated plainly with p-values]. These findings
[suggest/underscore/indicate] that [one-line interpretation tied to the aim
or pathophysiology].
```

Domain openers (rotate): "The present study enrolled…", "Laboratory analysis in the present study has revealed…", "Among [group], the mean [marker] in the present study was…", "Within the [group], [marker] demonstrated…", "[Analysis method] identified…", "ROC curve analysis in the present study yielded…", "In the present cohort, our [prognostic/correlative] finding showed…".

Interpretation closers (rotate): "These findings suggest that…", "These findings collectively underscore…", "The strong model performance underscores…", "These metrics position [marker] as…", "This parallel supports…", "The predominance of [pattern] in our cohort suggests that…".

## 3. Comparative paragraph (≤75 words per study, the signature block)

**Agreement:**
```
In agreement with our findings, **[Author] et al. ([Year])** ([ref]), in a
[design] of [N] [population] [at/in setting], reported [exact external values
with statistics and p-values], [link-back clause in words, no repetition of
own numbers].
```

Model (house standard, 66 words):
> In agreement with our findings, **Hu et al. (2026)** (5), in a cross-sectional study of 270 hospitalized T2DM patients stratified by MAFLD severity, reported no significant differences in sex distribution (males: 64.7% vs. 68.1% vs. 62.1%; p = 0.69), smoking prevalence (27.1% vs. 26.9% vs. 18.2%; p = 0.37), or blood pressure across groups (p > 0.05), echoing our finding of significantly elevated BMI in the steatotic group.

**Agreement with internal contrast:**
```
Also in line with our findings, **[Author] et al. ([Year])** ([ref]), in a
[design] of [N] [population], found [agreement values]. However, in contrast
to our findings, [divergent values], suggesting that [relationship] may vary
across [ethnic/clinical/design] settings.
```

**Contrast with attribution:**
```
In contrast to our findings, **[Author] et al. ([Year])** ([ref]) observed
[divergent values with statistics]. The [absence/direction] of [parameter]
significance in their cohort [may reflect / likely reflects / may be
attributed to] [population or design difference versus our cohort].
```

Model (house standard):
> In contrast to our findings, **Kurniawati et al. (2024)** (4) observed significantly elevated triglycerides in patients with hepatic steatosis (146 [45–654] vs. 112 [33–315] mg/dL; p = 0.011), but found no significant differences in FBG (p = 0.139), HbA1c (p = 0.058), or AST (p = 0.330) between groups. The absence of glycaemic significance in their cohort may reflect its predominantly outpatient, community-based composition compared to the more metabolically deranged inpatient-enriched sample in our study.

**Partial agreement:**
```
In partial agreement with our findings, **[Author] et al. ([Year])** ([ref])
reported [convergent values], while [divergent element] [contrasted with /
differed from] our [finding in words]. [Attribution if needed].
```

Link-back clause bank: "echoing our finding of…", "mirroring our…", "paralleling our own…", "closely concordant with our cohort", "reinforcing the association between…", "underscoring a comparable pattern of…", "aligning with our finding of…", "consistent with our observation of…", "closely aligning with our…", "substantiating…", "affirming… as…".

Discrepancy attribution bank (pick the true category, never invent):
- Population: "may reflect the [outpatient/community-based/older/more comorbid] composition of their cohort compared to our [setting] sample"
- Design: "likely reflects the shift in outcome ([X prediction] versus [Y detection])", "we used [design A], whereas they used [design B]"
- Sample size/power: "their smaller sample (n = [X] vs. our n = [Y]) provided less precision"
- Measurement: "[tool A] versus [tool B] as the outcome measure may explain the divergence"
- Heterogeneity (meta-analyses): "likely reflect the substantial heterogeneity inherent to meta-analytic pooling…, compared to our single-centre, diagnostically homogeneous design"

## 4. Mechanism paragraph

```
[Pathway topic sentence, present tense]. Specifically, [upstream factor]
functions as / activates / suppresses [mediator], [immediate consequence]
(refs). Because [established biochemical fact], this [axis/cascade] drives
[downstream event] (refs). Prior [in vitro/animal/bioinformatic] studies
[confirm/support] this mechanism in [context] (refs). [What this implies for
the present findings].
```

Certainty inserts: "Our data demonstrate…", "These findings suggest…", "A possible mechanism is…", "This implies that…", "We speculate that…".

Cascade notation (only when the chain is fully cited): `[A] → [B] → [C]` axis.

## 5. Summary (2 paragraphs, ~250–350 words)

Paragraph 1: burden and pathophysiology recap (present tense, cited sparingly because these refs already appeared).
Paragraph 2: diagnostic/management landscape, then close with: "This [design] study aimed to [aim] through [approach] of [N] participants across [groups]."

## 6. Summary of results (5–7 bullets)

Format per bullet: `**[Domain label]:** [one to two sentences with the key numbers].`
Domains: Study design & demographics / Clinical & laboratory profile / Staging or severity / Correlations / Independent predictors / Diagnostic or prognostic accuracy / Safety (if applicable).

## 7. Conclusions (single paragraph, match requested word count ±5)

```
This [design] study evaluated [exposure/tool] as [purpose] in [N] [population].
Compared to [controls], [group] exhibited [key findings with numbers]. [Second
key finding: correlation or predictor]. [Third: diagnostic accuracy numbers].
[Marker/approach] proved to be [evidence-based bottom line answering the aim].
```

Rules: numbers stay in, no new results or literature, active voice, strong final sentence, never "more research is needed".

## 8. Limitations (4–6 items, State → Impact → Mitigate)

```
* This study took place exclusively at [site]. This [single-centre/…] design
limits the ability to apply these findings and [derived parameter, e.g., the
cut-off ≥ X] to [broader populations], who have different [profiles/settings].
* The [cross-sectional/case-control] design prevents establishing [temporal or
causal] relationships between [exposure] and [outcome], and residual confounding
from unmeasured variables such as [examples] cannot be excluded. We attempted
to mitigate this by [adjustment/sensitivity analysis], which yielded consistent
results.
* The protocol based [diagnosis/grading] on [tool used] instead of [gold
standard]. This substitution introduces measurement limitations because
[tool-specific weakness].
* The study lacks [follow-up/longitudinal data]. This prevents assessment of
[what follow-up would have answered].
```

Omit any limitation that could not plausibly affect the results. Never apologetic.

## 9. Recommendations (4–6 items, specific)

```
* Base on the present study results, we recommend that [clinicians/settings]
integrate [tool] as [role] in [population], using [validated formula/parameters].
Practitioners may apply our identified [cut-off/threshold] to guide [action].
* Researchers need to conduct [design] with [population scope] to [validate/
confirm] [specific parameter] across [settings].
* Studies should directly compare [tool] against [named comparators] to define
its role in [clinical task].
* [Standardization/equity item]: establish [standard] across [platforms], and
evaluate [cost-effectiveness/access] in [resource-limited settings].
```

Each item: design + population + comparator + outcome + duration where applicable.

## 10. Tense and number quick tables

| Content | Tense |
|---|---|
| Own findings | Simple past |
| One prior study's finding | Simple past |
| General conclusion | Simple present |
| Established mechanism | Simple present |
| Field-level statement | Present perfect |
| Speculation | may / could / might |
| Recommendations | should / need to / may |

Numbers: spell 0–9 except with units; digits ≥10 and all units; leading zero (p = 0.047); exact p to 3 decimals; p < 0.001 only when smaller; every estimate with 95% CI; mean ± SD; median (range or IQR).

## 11. Transition bank

Addition: furthermore, moreover, in addition, also, additionally.
Contrast: however, nevertheless, conversely, in contrast, on the other hand.
Cause/effect: therefore, consequently, thus, accordingly, as a result.
Sequence: first, second, third, subsequently, finally, next.
Example: for example, for instance, such as.
Conclusion: in conclusion, in summary, overall, taken together.
Placement rule: sentence-initial for emphasis, mid-sentence for subtlety; never open two consecutive paragraphs with the same one.
