## 3. Style DNA (extracted from both samples)

### 3.1 Sentence-level signatures

Findings openers (rotate, never repeat mechanically):
- "The present study enrolled… / demonstrated… / revealed…"
- "Laboratory analysis in the present study has revealed…"
- "Among [group], the mean [marker] in the present study was…"
- "Within the [group], the [marker] demonstrated…"
- "Binomial logistic regression identified…"
- "ROC curve analysis in the present study yielded…"
- "In the present cohort, our prognostic finding showed…"
- "Interestingly, in the present work, …" / "Notably, …" (sparingly, one per block at most)

Interpretive closers:
- "These findings suggest that…"
- "These findings collectively underscore…"
- "This parallel supports…"
- "These metrics position [marker] as…"
- "The strong model performance underscores…"
- "Ultimately, these robust ROC metrics confirm…"
- "The predominance of… suggests that [condition] is frequently identified at early, potentially reversible stages…"

Stance openers for comparisons:
- Agreement: "In agreement with our findings,…" / "In harmony with our findings,…" / "In accordance with our data,…" / "In line with our findings,…" / "Also in line with our findings,…" / "Similarly,…" / "Further supporting these results,…" / "Moreover,…" / "Corroborating our findings,…" / "Likewise,…" / "Supporting our findings,…"
- Partial: "In partial agreement with our findings,…" / "In partial contrast to our findings,…"
- Contrast: "In contrast to our findings,…" / "However, in contrast to our findings,…"

Link-back clauses (the comparison's final move, words not numbers):
- "…echoing our finding of significantly elevated BMI in the steatotic group."
- "…mirroring our non-significant ALT result."
- "…paralleling our own non-significant age finding."
- "…values closely concordant with our cohort."
- "…closely paralleling our Treatment domain impairment."
- "…reinforcing the association between poor glycaemic control and hepatic steatosis."
- "…underscoring a comparable pattern of predominantly non-severe hepatic steatosis."
- "…aligning with our finding of a moderate rather than strong correlation."
- "…substantiating that Egypt's controlled elimination strategy has successfully suppressed transmission."

Discrepancy attribution (always methodological, never personal):
- "…may reflect its predominantly outpatient, community-based composition compared to the more metabolically deranged inpatient-enriched sample in our study."
- "…likely reflects population-specific metabolic profiles or differences in steatosis severity distribution compared to our sample."
- "…possibly reflecting the amplifying effect of T2DM on the relationship between insulin resistance and fibrogenesis."
- "This may be attributed to differences in the sample type, specifically the biological kinetics of RNA shedding in serum versus primary tissue biopsies, or the sample size of the evaluated cohort."
- "These lower estimates likely reflect the substantial heterogeneity inherent to meta-analytic pooling…, compared to our single-centre, diagnostically homogeneous design."
- "…suggesting that the relationship between [X] and [Y] may vary across different ethnic and clinical settings."
- Internal discrepancy: "This apparent discrepancy between [test A] and [test B] likely arises from [statistical property], and ultimately [resolution]."

Certainty ladder:
- Data-backed: "demonstrated", "established", "firmly confirm", "explicitly mediated by"
- Inference: "These findings suggest", "This implies", "This suggests"
- Possibility: "may play a regulatory role", "a possible mechanism is", "could serve as"
- Hypothesis: "We speculate that" (rare, once per chapter at most)

### 3.2 Statistical reporting conventions in the samples
- Groups compared with "vs." and stats inline: "53.2 ± 9.37 vs. 51.8 ± 8.40 years; p = 0.471"
- Medians with range or IQR in brackets: "median 8.90% vs. 8.00%; p < 0.001", "10.16 [7.54–15.01] vs. 7.8 [6.02–11.15] mmol/L"
- Exact p-values to three decimals; p < 0.001 when smaller
- ORs and estimates always with 95% CI: "OR: 1.51; 95% CI: 1.25–1.82; p = 0.001"
- ROC blocks report the full metric set: AUC with CI, cut-off, sensitivity and specificity with CIs, LR+, LR−, PPV, NPV, overall accuracy
- Non-significant results reported plainly and completely, never hidden

### 3.3 Paragraph rhythm
- Findings paragraphs: 4–7 sentences, ~100–150 words, ending with interpretation.
- Comparative paragraphs: 2–3 sentences, 60–80 words, one study each.
- The reader alternates between "our data" and "the field" every paragraph. This rhythm IS the house style.
- Longer sentences (30–45 words) carry parallel statistics; short sentences (5–10 words) deliver verdicts ("This parallel supports the utility of combined TyG and FIB-4 stratification.").

### 3.4 Citation formatting in the samples
- Numbered bold markers at sentence end before the period: "…among women worldwide **(1)**.", multiple: "**(6, 7)**"
- Author-prominent form for comparisons: "**Hu et al. (2026)** **(5)**," — in clean output render as: **Hu et al. (2026)** (5),
- Reference list: Vancouver numbered, journal abbreviations, year;volume(issue):pages

## 4. Why these two samples are the standard

1. **The alternating rhythm never breaks.** Every findings paragraph is answered by 1–4 comparison paragraphs in the same domain. Evidence and interpretation are inseparable.
2. **Numbers do the arguing.** Both samples report exact external values with p-values rather than vague claims of "similar findings".
3. **Contrast is always explained, never left raw.** Every disagreement ends with a methodological or biological attribution in the same paragraph.
4. **Non-significant results are kept visible** and given mechanistic or methodological explanations rather than being dropped.
5. **Mechanism is layered, not dumped.** RL-72 builds the ROR → miR-205 → ZEB1/2 → CDH1 → EMT cascade one cited step at a time, then uses it to resolve the soluble E-cadherin paradox.
6. **Conclusions quantify and close.** The Salama conclusion restates design, findings, correlation, regression, and ROC numbers in one paragraph and lands on a bottom line, not a plea for more research.
7. **Limitations and recommendations are concrete.** Single centre, cross-sectional design, ultrasound instead of biopsy, no follow-up — each with its impact stated. Recommendations carry formula, cut-off, comparator sets, and target populations.

## 5. Failure patterns in weaker samples (avoid)

- Opening the Discussion by re-narrating the Introduction instead of framing burden-gap-aim.
- Comparative paragraphs that summarize a paper for 150+ words with no link-back to the study.
- Unexplained contrast ("Jones found different results.") left hanging.
- Repeating the study's own numbers inside comparative paragraphs instead of referring to them in words.
- Interpretation-free findings paragraphs (data with no "These findings suggest…" close).
- Conclusions that trail off with "more research is needed" or recycle the summary verbatim.
- Limitations as a single apologetic sentence ("Unfortunately, our sample was small.") instead of State → Impact → Mitigate items.
- Citation dumping: stacking 4+ refs at one sentence end with no differentiation.
- Inconsistent tense: drifting into present tense for the study's own findings.
- Robustness words on autopilot: "robust", "profound", "notably", "interestingly" opening half the paragraphs (the best samples use each sparingly).
