# Section blueprints

Paragraph-level blueprints for the nine-block journal variant. Each block states its job, its paragraph budget, and a skeleton.

Word budget: 1,500–3,000 total. Do not inflate a thin evidence base with repetition; a 5-trial review can be excellent at 1,800 words.

---

## Block 1, Opening synthesis and principal findings (1–2 paragraphs, 200–350 words)

**Job**: answer the review question immediately, establish the evidence scope, acknowledge the full outcome set.

**Sentence order**:

1. Design + k + N + population + primary direction and magnitude.
2. Whether the objective was answered, and with what certainty.
3. Secondary outcomes in one sentence, including null domains.
4. Safety in one sentence, including the absence of a signal if true.
5. The clinical takeaway in one sentence, without overreach.

**Skeleton**:

> In this systematic review and meta-analysis of [k] [designs] involving [N] [population], [intervention] [reduced/increased] [primary outcome] by [magnitude] at [timepoint], answering our primary objective. The direction was consistent across [regions/settings], and the effect was already evident at [early timepoint]. [Secondary outcome 1] and [secondary outcome 2] favoured [arm], whereas [null domain] showed little or no difference. [Safety domain] did not reveal a clinically important penalty, although the evidence for [rare event] was of [certainty]. These findings [support/qualify] [clinical action] in [population].

**Never open with**: disease burden, prevalence, pathophysiology, or `Hypertension is a major public health problem`.

---

## Block 2, Time-course efficacy (2–4 paragraphs, 500–800 words)

**Job**: interpret the trajectory and the pharmacology. Cover MSSBP and MSDBP at every analysed timepoint.

**Paragraph 2a, trajectory**: onset at the earliest timepoint, amplification to the primary timepoint, plateau or continued decline. Compare against the expected pharmacodynamic onset of each class.

**Paragraph 2b, mechanism**: why the three classes complement each other.

- Diuretic: natriuresis, plasma-volume reduction, longer half-life, late vasodilation.
- Dihydropyridine CCB: arteriolar vasodilation, reduced peripheral resistance.
- ARB: AT1 blockade, reduced aldosterone and vasoconstriction, blunting of compensatory RAAS activation.

State each mechanism as established physiology with a citation. Do not present a mechanism as a measured result of the review.

**Paragraph 2c, agent and comparator differences**: why specific ARBs or dual backbones might differ. Keep to the four discrepancy categories.

**Paragraph 2d, clinical meaning**: compare the pooled difference with the MCID and with landmark trial differences. A 2 mmHg difference is population-relevant; a 10 mmHg difference is individually actionable.

**Example of a mechanism sentence**:

> Chlorthalidone maintains natriuresis across a longer dosing interval than hydrochlorothiazide, and the accompanying RAAS activation is blunted by the ARB component, so the combination limits the counter-regulatory response that erodes diuretic monotherapy over time.

---

## Block 3, Target attainment (1–2 paragraphs, 250–400 words)

**Job**: interpret control and response rates as absolute, threshold-dependent outcomes.

Include:

- the threshold used by the trials (`<140/90` or `<130/80`);
- pooled RR or OR plus the absolute difference where derivable;
- the early timepoints and why early control matters (therapeutic inertia, adherence, early risk);
- the distinction between response (a defined fall) and control (a target reached).

Avoid claiming a patient-level benefit from a pooled RR alone. If the absolute difference is not reported and cannot be derived without inventing data, say the review did not report it.

---

## Block 4, Comparison with prior reviews and landmark trials (1–2 paragraphs, 300–500 words)

**Job**: position the review. This is where novelty is claimed, with evidence.

**Paragraph 4a, prior reviews**: block synthesis, not a list. Group prior reviews by scope (thiazide-like vs any thiazide; triple vs dual; network vs pairwise) and state whether this review confirms, contradicts, or extends each group.

**Paragraph 4b, landmark trials**: compare effect magnitude and design. Name the specific difference: agent, dose, comparator, population, follow-up, endpoint definition.

**Novelty sentence patterns**:

- `Unlike earlier syntheses that pooled hydrochlorothiazide with thiazide-like diuretics, this review restricted inclusion to Chlorthalidone-based regimens, which reduces a known source of pharmacological heterogeneity.`
- `This review adds week-by-week estimates at Weeks 2, 4, and 8, a granularity that earlier pooled analyses did not report.`
- `The inclusion of [newer trials] increases the pooled sample by [N] and extends coverage to [population].`

Never claim novelty without naming the prior work it exceeds.

---

## Block 5, Safety, tolerability, and laboratory profile (1–2 paragraphs, 300–500 words)

**Job**: treat safety as a primary result, not an afterthought.

Cover, separately:

- total AEs, SAEs, ADRs, discontinuations due to AEs;
- symptomatic hypotension and dizziness;
- peripheral oedema and the ARB-CCB interaction;
- electrolyte shifts (potassium, sodium);
- metabolic and renal parameters (uric acid, glucose, creatinine/eGFR).

**Mechanism examples**:

> Adding an ARB to a dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker attenuates CCB-induced precapillary arteriolar dilation through postcapillary venular dilation, which lowers capillary hydrostatic pressure and reduces dependent oedema.

> RAAS blockade reduces aldosterone-driven renal potassium wasting, so the ARB component partially offsets the hypokalaemic effect of Chlorthalidone.

> A small early rise in creatinine can reflect reduced intraglomerular pressure rather than structural injury; the distinction requires the trajectory over time, not a single value.

State the power limitation for rare events. A pooled analysis of five trials cannot exclude a rare serious adverse event.

---

## Block 6, Clinical implications and guideline alignment (1 paragraph, 200–350 words)

**Job**: convert evidence into a defensible clinical action.

Include:

- the candidate population, named precisely;
- the step-up logic relative to dual therapy;
- the role of single-pill combinations in adherence and therapeutic inertia;
- alignment with current guideline recommendations, cited;
- the monitoring plan (electrolytes, renal function, uric acid) with a time window;
- cost, availability, and equity considerations.

Calibrate strength to certainty. `Should` only for high or moderate certainty.

---

## Block 7, Strengths and limitations (2 paragraphs, 300–450 words)

**Paragraph 7a, strengths**: registration, database coverage, dual screening, PRISMA 2020, risk-of-bias tools (RoB 2, ROBINS-I, ROBIS, AMSTAR-2), pre-specified subgroups, certainty assessment, timepoint granularity.

**Paragraph 7b, limitations**: Tier 1 study-level, then Tier 2 review-level. Each with State → Impact → Mitigate.

**Skeleton**:

> This review has several limitations. At the study level, [limitation]. This may [impact on validity or precision]. We [mitigation], although [residual uncertainty]. At the review level, [limitation]. This [impact on generalisability]. [Mitigation or explicit acceptance].

No `unfortunately`, no apology, no listing a limitation that cannot plausibly affect the conclusion.

---

## Block 8, Future research (1 paragraph, 150–250 words)

**Job**: a concrete agenda, not a lament.

Four coordinates per item: population, intervention/comparator, design, outcome/duration.

**Skeleton**:

> Several questions remain. A multicentre double-blind randomised trial in [underrepresented population] comparing [intervention] with [active comparator] over [duration] is needed to determine whether the pooled blood-pressure difference translates into fewer [hard outcomes]. Future work should report 24-hour ambulatory pressure, including nocturnal dipping, given the long half-life of [agent]. Head-to-head single-pill comparisons between [class A] and [class B] would clarify whether the class difference observed here is pharmacological or formulation-driven. Pragmatic and registry-based studies should estimate adherence, cost-effectiveness, and safety in routine care.

Each sentence names a design, a population, and an outcome. No sentence says only `more research is needed`.

---

## Block 9, Conclusions (1 paragraph, 120–200 words)

1. Design, population, objective.
2. Primary result with anchor estimate.
3. Key secondary and safety result.
4. Certainty-calibrated bottom line.
5. One clinical implication.
6. Strong close.

**Skeleton**:

> In adults with uncontrolled hypertension, Chlorthalidone-based triple therapy with amlodipine and an ARB lowered Week 8 mean sitting systolic pressure by [magnitude] more than dual therapy, with consistent reductions from Week 2 and higher control and response rates. Serious adverse events did not differ materially, and the laboratory profile was consistent with the known effects of the component classes. On [certainty] evidence, this combination [shows/likely/may] offers a rapid and well-tolerated step-up option for patients who remain above target on dual therapy. The decision should remain individualised, with early monitoring of electrolytes and renal function.

No new citations, no new numbers, no generic research call.

---

## Worked cardiovascular example (Chlorthalidone + amlodipine + ARB)

This is the reference case the pack was built around. Treat it as a template, not as evidence for another study.

- **Population**: adults with uncontrolled hypertension on dual therapy.
- **Intervention**: Chlorthalidone + amlodipine + an ARB, as a free combination or single pill.
- **Comparator**: any dual combination of those classes.
- **Primary outcome**: change in mean sitting systolic pressure at Week 8.
- **Secondary**: systolic at Weeks 2 and 4; diastolic at Weeks 2, 4, 8; control and response rates at all three timepoints.
- **Safety**: AEs, SAEs, ADRs, discontinuations, potassium, sodium, uric acid, creatinine/eGFR, glucose at Week 8.

Comparator classes to map externally: olmesartan/amlodipine/HCTZ, valsartan/amlodipine/HCTZ, perindopril/indapamide/amlodipine, low-dose triple single-pill regimens, and Chlorthalidone-based dual combinations. The discussion should state explicitly whether the comparison is class-level or molecule-level.

Do not transfer the example's numbers to a different study. Every number must come from that study's own files.
